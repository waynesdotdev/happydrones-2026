An empty building doesn't stay the same. Gutters fill, slates slip, lead goes missing, windows get broken and rubbish gets dumped. Nobody's there to notice, so small problems become expensive ones. A monthly drone visit is a simple way to keep an eye on it.

## What changes in an empty building

Drag the [month slider](/vacant-property-monitoring/) on the vacant property page and you'll see a typical year: plants in the gutter by month 2, slipped slates by month 3, stolen lead by month 5, then broken windows, fly-tipping, a fence down and graffiti. None of it is unusual. All of it is cheaper to deal with early.

## What the drone checks

- Roof slopes and the ridge, from a few metres away
- Chimneys and lead flashing, a common target for theft
- Gutters and downpipes
- Windows and boarding
- Doors and other ways in
- Walls, fences, gates and the yard

## Why the same positions every month

Every visit flies the same positions and heights, so month 9 lines up with month 1. New damage stands out straight away, and you have a dated record of when it appeared.

## What you get

A dated report within 48–72 hours: new issues first, with numbered photos and where they are on the building, then older issues listed as still open or fixed. If something looks urgent, like an open roof or signs someone's been getting in, I'll message you the same day.

## What it doesn't do

A drone looks from outside. It doesn't replace the inside-and-outside checks most insurers ask for on empty buildings, and it isn't a structural survey. It adds the parts nobody can see from the ground, with a dated photo record to go with your own logs.
