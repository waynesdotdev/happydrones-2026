Weather decides more drone shoots than anything else. So when you plan a job with me now, you can check the forecast for your date before you even send it.

## Why the weather matters so much

A drone can fly in more than you'd think, but good results need the right conditions:

- **Wind:** it's usually windier 120 metres up than in your garden. Strong gusts mean shaky footage, and past a certain point it isn't safe to fly at all.
- **Rain:** drones and rain don't mix, and wet lenses ruin photos.
- **Light:** in December the sun sets before 4pm in Teesside. A 5pm shoot then would be in the dark.

I've always checked all of this before every job. Now you can see it too.

## What the weather check shows

On the [mission planner](/plan/), fill in your postcode, date, name and email, then tap **Check the weather for my date** in step 07. You'll see:

1. **A verdict for your date:** Looks good, Some risk, or Likely to move.
2. **A rough best time of day,** based on the lightest wind and lowest chance of rain while it's light.
3. **An hour-by-hour chart.** Green bars are good flying, amber is some risk, red is poor. Hours before dawn and after dusk are greyed out, so you can see straight away when it's dark.
4. **Your own time.** Tap any hour to pick it as your preferred time, and it tells you how that hour looks: the sky, the gusts, the chance of rain and the temperature.
5. **Sunrise, sunset and golden light,** the warm, low sun just after sunrise and before sunset that makes houses look their best.

![An example weather check: a good day with 10:00 picked](/posts/weather-check-example-good.webp)

## If your date looks poor

If wind or rain look likely to spoil your date, the check suggests **up to three better dates** in the forecast. One tap moves your plan to that day.

![An example of a poor day, with better dates suggested underneath](/posts/weather-check-example-poor.webp)

## What it can and can't tell you

- **It's a guide, not a promise.** It uses the Met Office forecast for your postcode area, checked against my drone's wind and weather limits. Forecasts change, especially a few days out.
- **It reaches about a week ahead.** Book further out and it'll say the forecast isn't ready yet. You can still book that date.
- **I make the final call.** I check the forecast again before every shoot. If the weather turns, your date moves to the next good one at no cost, as it says in my [booking agreement](/terms/).

## Why it asks for your name and email first

The forecast comes from the Met Office, and there's a limit on how many checks the site can make each day. Asking for your details first keeps it working for real customers, not bots. Your details only go to me with your plan.
