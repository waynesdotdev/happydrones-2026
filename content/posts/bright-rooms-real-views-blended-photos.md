Ever seen a listing where the room looks fine but the windows are glowing white? Or the view looks great but the room is dark? That's the camera hitting its limit. Here's how I get around it.

## The problem: one photo can't see everything

Your eyes adjust instantly between a bright window and a darker room. A camera can't. In a single photo, it has to choose:

- expose for the room, and the windows blow out to white
- expose for the window, and the room goes dark

## The fix: three exposures, blended

For each angle, I take three photos one after another: one darker, one normal, one brighter. Then I blend them, so the room comes from the brighter photo and the view through the window from the darker one.

The result looks like what you'd see standing in the room: bright and welcoming, with the garden or the view still visible outside. You can drag the [blend slider](/property-photography/) on the property photography page to compare one exposure with the blended photo.

## What I don't do

Blending fixes light, not the house. I never:

- remove damp, cracks or anything a buyer should know about
- change the size or shape of rooms
- add furniture that isn't there

Buyers should walk in and recognise the photos. That's good for them, and good for the agent.

## Where it matters most

- Rooms with big windows or patio doors
- Homes with a view: sea, countryside or a garden
- Dark rooms that need every bit of light
