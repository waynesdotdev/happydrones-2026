Lots of people take progress photos. Most of them don't line up: a different angle, a different height, a different time of day. Lay them side by side and it's hard to tell what's actually changed. That's why every construction visit I fly uses **the same fixed positions**.

## What fixed positions are

On the first visit, I agree a set of positions with you: a top-down view of the whole site, plus views from each side at set heights and angles. Each one is recorded, and every visit after that flies exactly the same ones.

You can see an example on the [capture plan](/construction/), where each position shows its height, angle and what it's for.

## Why it matters

**Month 1 lines up with month 9.** Because every photo is taken from the same spot, you can flick between visits and see exactly what's been built, moved or finished.

**Dated evidence.** If there's ever a dispute about when something was done, or what state the site was in, a dated photo from the same position is hard to argue with.

**Reports write themselves.** Funders, clients and head office want to see progress. A set of matching photos says more than a page of notes.

**Marketing for later.** The same photos turn into a time-lapse film of the site taking shape, ready for the sales launch.

## What you get after each visit

- Fixed-position aerial stills from the same points every visit
- A top-down overview of the whole site
- A short progress film
- A dated, organised folder per visit, within 48–72 hours

## How often?

Monthly suits most schemes. Fortnightly works for fast-moving stages and fit-outs. Repeat visits cost less each: progress visits start at £179, with 5–15% off for regular bookings.
