It's one of the questions I get asked most, often by a neighbour while I'm setting up: "Are you allowed to fly that over my house?" Here's the honest answer, in plain English.

## The short answer

There's no blanket rule against a drone flying over a house. In the UK, nobody owns the sky above their home at any height: the law says a flight at a reasonable height isn't trespass on its own. What drone pilots *are* bound by is the Civil Aviation Authority's rules, which cover how high, how close and how safely they fly, and privacy law, which covers what they film.

## How high can a drone go?

No higher than **120 metres (400 feet)** above the ground. That's the limit for everyday drone flying, and it keeps drones well below planes and helicopters.

## How close can it fly to people and homes?

It depends on the drone:

- **Small drones** (under 250 grams, or marked UK0, UK1 or C0, which includes the DJI Mini 4 Pro I use for most jobs) can fly over and near people who aren't involved, and in residential areas, as long as they're flown safely. They must never fly over crowds.
- **Bigger drones** have to keep at least 50 metres from people who aren't involved, and 150 metres from residential, commercial and recreational areas.

Whatever the size, the pilot is responsible for flying safely.

## Does the pilot need a licence?

Anyone flying a drone of 100 grams or more needs a **Flyer ID** from the CAA, which means passing a theory test. Most drones with a camera also need an **Operator ID**, and the operator's number must be on the drone. People flying for work also need insurance by law.

I hold both, plus the A2 Certificate of Competency, a higher qualification. I'm insured with £10 million public liability as a minimum on every job.

## What about privacy?

The drone rules say to respect people's privacy, and anyone filming for a business has to follow data protection law. In practice, a careful pilot:

- plans flights to point the camera at the job, not at neighbours' gardens and windows
- avoids filming people who aren't involved
- doesn't publish photos where people can be identified without good reason

That's how I fly every job. If I'm working next door to you, I'm happy to show you what I'm filming.

## Worried about a drone?

- **Talk to the pilot** if you can see them. Most are happy to explain.
- **If it's dangerous or criminal**, such as flying over a crowd, near an airport, or harassing someone, call the police on 101, or 999 in an emergency.

## The bottom line

A drone over your house isn't automatically breaking the law, but its pilot has rules to follow, and you're entitled to ask about them. A professional should be happy to answer.
