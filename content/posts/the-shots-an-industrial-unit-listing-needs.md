Selling or letting an industrial unit is different from selling a house. Occupiers care about access, yard space, loading and location far more than how the reception looks. These are the shots I plan every commercial listing around. You can watch them build up on the [flyover](/commercial-property/), where the camera rises from the frontage to the whole site.

## 1. The frontage

The unit from the front, at eye level and slightly raised, showing signage, the main entrance and the condition of the building.

## 2. The yard and loading

From above: how much yard space there is, where the loading doors are, how lorries turn and where staff park. For many occupiers, this is the first thing they check.

## 3. Access

The route in from the main road: gates, the estate road, turning space. A raised shot along the access answers "can an artic get in?" before anyone asks.

## 4. The whole site in its surroundings

A higher shot showing the unit, its neighbours and the estate. It shows scale, and whether there's room to grow.

## 5. Location context

The big one for commercial property: how close the site is to the main roads, motorway junctions, rail and ports. On the [context map](/commercial-property/) you can switch these on and off to see what an annotated location aerial shows. Drive-time labels on a real photo sell a location faster than a paragraph of text.

## Inside

Clear-height shots of the warehouse space and photos of the offices and welfare areas, flown or filmed where it's safe and useful.
