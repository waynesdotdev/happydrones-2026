People often assume aerial photos are taken as high as possible. In fact, most of the best ones aren't. Every height shows something different, and choosing the right one is half the skill. You can drag the [height slider](/aerial-photography/) on the aerial photography page to see the same house change as the drone climbs.

## Low: 3–10 metres

Just above head height to roof level. **Shows:** the front of a house from a slightly raised angle, roof details close up, a garden from just above the fence. **Used for:** the "hero" shot of a house, which looks natural, not like a map, and for close roof and gutter checks.

## Medium: 15–40 metres

Roughly the height of a tall tree to a small block of flats. **Shows:** the whole property and plot in one frame, with the shape of the building clear. **Used for:** the opening aerial on a listing, the plot from above, and roof overviews.

## High: 50–120 metres

Up to the legal limit of 120 metres. **Shows:** the property in its setting: the street, the park, the coast, the roads in. **Used for:** location shots, land and plots, construction site overviews, and showing how close a home is to what matters.

## Straight down

A camera pointing straight down at any height. **Shows:** layout, boundaries and plot shape, like a map. **Used for:** construction progress overviews, land boundaries and roof overviews with numbered markers.

## Why not always go high?

From 120 metres a house becomes a small rectangle. Buyers can't see the front door, the windows or the garden. Most property aerials look best between 15 and 40 metres, slightly angled, so you still see the face of the building.
