On a property portal, a listing has a second or two to stop someone scrolling. After that, the photos have to do the work of a first viewing. These are the five shots I plan every property shoot around.

## 1. The opening aerial

The first photo is the one buyers see in the search results, so it should show the whole property at once: the house, the plot and how it sits. A slightly raised angle, not straight down, looks natural and shows the shape of the home.

## 2. The plot from above

Buyers want to know how big the garden really is, where the boundaries run and what's behind the fence. A shot from higher up answers that in one picture, especially for gardens, driveways, land and outbuildings.

## 3. The setting

Where is it? An aerial looking out from the property shows the street, nearby green space, the village or the view. It sells the location, which is something a ground photo can't do.

## 4. The front, at eye level

Aerials don't replace a good ground shot of the front door. Taken straight on, in good light, it's the photo buyers picture themselves walking up to. Bright cloud or low evening sun both work; harsh midday shadows don't.

## 5. The walkthrough glide

A smooth, one-take tour from the front door through the main rooms and out into the garden, at walking height. It's filmed on a handheld stabilised camera, not a drone, and it gives buyers a real feel for the layout before they book a viewing. Agents can add the link as the video tour on their listing.

## Putting it together

You don't need all five for every home. A flat might only need the setting and the walkthrough; a detached house with land needs the aerials most.
