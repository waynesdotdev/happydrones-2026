Roofers talk about ridges, valleys, flashing and verges. If you're not sure what any of that means, here's a plain-English guide to the parts of a roof, and what tends to go wrong with each. You can also tap your way round the [roof explorer](/roof-inspections/), which shows each part from above, just as the drone sees it.

## Slopes (the main roof)

The big sloping areas covered in tiles or slates. **What fails:** tiles or slates slip, crack or go missing, usually after high winds. Moss build-up can lift them too.

## Ridge

The line along the very top, where two slopes meet, capped with ridge tiles. **What fails:** the mortar holding ridge tiles cracks and washes out, and tiles loosen. It takes the worst of the weather.

## Hips

On some roofs, the sloping edges where two slopes meet at a corner. **What fails:** much the same as the ridge: loose hip tiles and failing mortar.

## Valleys

The inside corner where two slopes meet and water runs down. **What fails:** blockages from leaves and moss, and worn valley linings, which can let water in.

## Chimney and flashing

The flashing is the lead (or similar) that seals the gap where the chimney meets the roof. **What fails:** flashing lifts, cracks or gets stolen; pots crack; brickwork and pointing wear; plants take root on top.

## Gutters and downpipes

**What fails:** blockages, sagging runs and dropped joints. Overflowing gutters are one of the most common causes of damp walls.

## Verges and fascias

The verge is the edge of the roof at the gable end. Fascias are the boards along the bottom edge that hold the gutters. **What fails:** loose verge tiles or mortar, and rotten or damaged boards. The wind gets in here first.

## Flat roofs

Felt, rubber or fibreglass, often on extensions and garages. **What fails:** standing water, splits, blisters and lifting edge trims. They usually need attention sooner than pitched roofs.
