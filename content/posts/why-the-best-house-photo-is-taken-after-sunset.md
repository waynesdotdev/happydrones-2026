Scroll any property portal and the listings that stop you are often the dusk shots: the house lit up from inside against a deep blue sky. They're not taken at sunset. They're taken just after it, in a short window photographers call blue hour.

## What blue hour is

After the sun sets, the sky doesn't go dark straight away. For a short while it turns a deep, even blue. Put the house lights on and the windows glow warm against it. The contrast between warm windows and cool sky is what makes the photo.

You can watch it happen on the [dusk slider](/property-photography/): drag from mid-afternoon to blue hour and see the house change.

## Why it sells

- **It feels like home.** Lit windows say "someone lives a happy life here".
- **It stands out.** Most listings are shot in the middle of the day, so a dusk shot is different.
- **It hides less-than-perfect skies.** A grey day often turns into a lovely blue hour.

## When to book it

- **Timing:** about 15–30 minutes after sunset. In Teesside that's around 4pm in December and nearer 10pm in June.
- **Weather:** a clear or partly cloudy evening is best. The weather check on my [planner](/plan/) shows sunset times for your date.
- **On the night:** every light on, curtains open, and cars off the drive.

## Which homes suit it

Homes with lots of windows at the front, garden lighting, or a striking design look best. It works on almost any home, though: a lit window always draws the eye.
